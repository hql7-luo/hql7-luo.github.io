Re-plan tonight's orders for 50 grocery items, right in your browser.

Walmart store CA_1 · 50 FOODS_3 items · 83 evenings, Feb 29 to May 21, 2016

Sales and shelf prices are real (M5 data). Costs and the delivery and storage limits are assumptions. Stock on hand and orders in transit on each evening come from the project's 84-day LP backtest.

Pick an evening and set the store's limits. A linear program (the HiGHS solver, compiled to WebAssembly) plans the next six evenings of orders for every item. Then replay the next 7 days on actual sales and compare it with a standard order-up-to rule.

How it works, and its limits

  • Forecast: Holt-Winters (damped trend, weekly season), refit every evening on the last 364 days. Its daily error is 33.8%, against 39.4% for a 7-day moving average.
  • Plan: one linear program for all 50 items over 7 days. It pays the lost margin on forecast demand it cannot meet and on the shortage expected from forecast error (a piecewise-linear normal loss curve), plus holding and $0.10 for each delivered unit that does not fit.
  • Limits: units delivered per day, and units on hand after a delivery, shared by all items.
  • The same model runs in Python in the project. On six test evenings the browser and Python reach the same optimal cost.
  • Recorded sales stand in for demand, and they understate it when an item was out of stock. Costs and limits are assumptions.
