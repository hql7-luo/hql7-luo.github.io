Chinese Consumer Brands Going Global

Pop Mart, MINISO and Mixue all went global. Whose model travels best?

A strategy comparison built on company filings from FY2023 to the first half of 2026: overseas revenue, store growth, store model and margins.

The short answer

MINISO's model is the most replicable: 113 countries and regions, overseas revenue up in every period, and a staged entry model that does not need a hit product.

Pop Mart is the most profitable but the hardest to copy: its overseas revenue fell 11.1% in 1H2026 as LABUBU cooled. Mixue's overseas store count has shrunk for three half-years in a row.

What I did

I chose the question, the three companies and the comparison approach: overseas revenue share, store growth, store model and gross margin. Claude substantially assisted the research, code and writing.

Business problem

Going global is the hot topic. Which playbook can actually be repeated?

Pop Mart, MINISO and Mixue are three of the most visible Chinese consumer brands abroad, with very different models: collectible IP toys, low-price design retail and franchised tea and ice cream.

Fast growth abroad does not show whether a model keeps working in new markets, or whether other Chinese brands could learn it. I wanted to separate "replicable" from "profitable", because they can point to different winners.

What I did

My Approach

  1. Collect overseas revenue, store counts, operating models and margins from annual and interim results announcements, FY2023 to 1H2026, with a source for every number.
  2. Put the three on comparable terms: each company's own definition of overseas, first half compared with first half, and revenue per store by business model.
  3. Score each company from 1 to 5 on six dimensions, from market breadth to profitability, with the evidence written next to every score.
  4. Test the ranking with 10,000 random weightings, and recheck 13 numbers the companies report themselves.

Mixue does not report overseas revenue, so its overseas picture relies on store counts.

Lessons

What Other Brands Can Learn

For another Chinese brand going abroad, MINISO's playbook is the one to study: export the supply chain, not just the low price, and match the entry model to the market, with distributors for reach, partners in the middle and own stores where the brand matters.

Treat a hit IP as an accelerator, not a system, and track same-store sales and partner inventory, not just store count. MINISO's overseas stores grew while same-store GMV slipped and overseas inventory turnover reached 273 days.

What would change my view

  • Pop Mart: another IP carries overseas growth without LABUBU.
  • MINISO: North America reaches the ~10% net margin management targets for 2026, or distributor closures spread beyond weak markets.
  • Mixue: Indonesia and Vietnam stop shrinking, and new markets such as Central Asia, Mexico and Brazil start to scale.

Industry analysis based on public filings; not investment advice.

Methods & limitations

How the comparison was built

Overseas follows each company's own reporting: Pop Mart's Asia Pacific, Americas and Europe (excluding HK, Macau and Taiwan from FY2024), MINISO-brand overseas markets, and Mixue stores outside mainland China.

Six dimensions scored 1 to 5: proven in many markets, not reliant on one hit, partners fund the stores, other brands can learn it, latest overseas trend and profitability (adjusted net margin bands). The headline ranking uses equal weights.

Checks: a script recomputes 13 figures the companies report themselves (growth rates, margins, store totals) and confirms they match.

Scope & limitations

The scorecard is an analyst judgment, made transparent, not a statistical model. Mixue does not disclose overseas revenue or margins. MINISO's overseas revenue mixes retail and wholesale, so revenue per store is not like-for-like. Pop Mart changed its regional definition in FY2024.
