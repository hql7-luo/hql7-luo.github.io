Business question

Who should receive which email, under a limited budget?

A retailer needs to know whether marketing creates purchases that would not otherwise happen—not just which customers are likely to buy. I translated this into campaign effects, customer heterogeneity and equal-budget policy comparisons.

The real experiment assigns 21,307 customers to Men’s Email, 21,387 to Women’s Email and 21,306 to No Email. Outcomes are visits, purchases and spend. The decision metric is measured per assigned or eligible customer, not just per responder.

All 64,000 rows are retained. There are no missing cells; 6,562 indistinguishable full rows are reported, not deleted, because the source has no customer identifier. Merchandise indicators describe prior purchases, not customer gender.

Evidence and alternatives

What earns the next marketing dollar?

The six charts come from the same reproducible pipeline. Full-sample campaign effects, exploratory segment findings and untouched-test policy estimates answer different questions and keep separate uncertainty statements.

Charts use English labels; findings, units, assumptions and decisions are explained in both page languages. Expand any chart for legible detail.

Analytical design

Make the decision traceable to the experiment.

Visible SQL and Python audit customer profiles and assigned outcomes. Campaign comparisons estimate intent-to-treat differences in conversion and spend, using confidence intervals and Holm correction within each three-contrast outcome family. Maximum pre-treatment standardized imbalance is 0.0169.

An arm-stratified, fixed-seed 32,000/32,000 split separates policy learning from evaluation. No treatment, visit, conversion or post-email spend is used as a targeting feature. The six-segment rule and control logistic baseline are fitted on training data; no test-set retuning is performed.

Held-out policy values use Horvitz–Thompson weighting with documented assignment probability 1/3, subtracting the same eligible population’s no-email counterfactual. Paired policy differences use the same test customers. Policy intervals are conditional on the learned rule and omit training instability.

Recommendation and next test

Run the simplest defensible pilot first.

Keep Men’s Email as the campaign benchmark. For strict capacity, random Men is a credible low-complexity comparator; the learned segment rule has not demonstrated superiority. Retain a randomized No Email group and do not present historical estimates as realized business growth.

A current experiment should compare random Men, the frozen segment rule and No Email at equal budget, randomizing within customer strata. Record actual contribution margin, delivery, unsubscribes and a longer value horizon. Size it from a management-approved minimum effect; freeze the primary contrast, multiplicity plan and stop rules before assignment.

What the evidence cannot establish

The data is historical, has no customer IDs, and contains only 578 buyers and two weeks of outcomes. There is no actual email cost, margin, unsubscribe or fatigue measure. Exploratory segment findings and conditional policy intervals do not prove individual uplift, long-run profit or current-market transferability. The retrospective analysis plan is not a prospective preregistration.

No explicit source-data redistribution license was located. This case publishes only aggregate evidence and generated charts; raw and customer-level derived records remain excluded. The code license does not grant rights to the original data.
