Which products should a retailer check first when stock keeps running out?

Stockouts can mean missed sales. This project helps retailers focus checks and compare forecasts; recorded sales alone cannot reveal unmet demand.

What should the business do?

Review repeated stockouts in high-contribution products first. Use the simple forecast as a benchmark, and verify actual stock and supplier inputs before replenishing.

Turn broad alerts into a manageable review

Many stockout alerts do not tell a manager where to spend limited review time. This project connects recorded stockouts with sales contribution, then compares forecasts for planning.

Four findings, four practical next steps

These are proposed responsibilities for a retail team, not actions already carried out by a company. All findings below use the full dataset.

Investigate repeated stockouts

Finding
40,591 of 50,000 series (81.2%) are Critical or High Risk in the latest 28 days.
Why it matters
A broad alert list needs a manageable investigation scope; severity alone is not a purchase quantity.
Suggested owner and action
Store operations or an inventory analyst: use the severity list to investigate availability, active assortment and recording issues.
Inputs needed before acting
Actual shelf and on-hand stock, replenishment logs, active-assortment status and stockout-record checks.

Focus limited review capacity

Finding
122 of 865 product IDs contribute at least 80% of normalized observed sales over the 97 days.
Why it matters
Concentration helps focus attention, but observed volume is not revenue, profit or recovered demand.
Suggested owner and action
Category manager: review high-contribution items with repeated stockouts using the existing sales-sorted list.
Inputs needed before acting
Current stock, open orders, supplier constraints, and actual margin or spoilage information if financial decisions are needed.

Keep a simple forecast benchmark

Finding
The fixed exponential-smoothing baseline has 36.0% pooled WAPE; per-series model selection has 37.4% on the same final week. Lower is better.
Why it matters
Additional model selection did not improve this holdout. A simpler benchmark should remain visible.
Suggested owner and action
Demand planner: keep the simple method as a benchmark and evaluate future periods before adopting a forecasting policy.
Inputs needed before acting
New observed sales, stockout flags, a predefined comparison window and measured supplier lead times for subsequent planning.

Check availability before removing an item

Finding
76.4% of zero-sales days also record stockouts.
Why it matters
Zero sales may reflect availability or recording issues. Coexistence does not prove their cause or reveal hidden demand.
Suggested owner and action
Store operations and the category manager: verify whether an item is available and still offered before proposing an assortment change.
Inputs needed before acting
Actual availability, active-assortment status, sales-record quality, and additional demand evidence.

Two review questions, using existing views

The risk thresholds and saved classifications stay unchanged. Changing display order does not create a new forecast, risk score or optimization model.

Severity list: what needs diagnosis?

Filter to Critical / High Risk and retain the original severity priority: risk, stockout-hour exposure, affected-day share, variability, then series ID. Use it to investigate availability, assortment and recording.

Contribution list: where should review effort focus?

A replenishment planner can inspect Critical / High Risk in the local Streamlit sales view, sorted by 97-day observed sales, and review the saved store–product A class. An A-only worklist is a documented filter on those saved fields, not an implemented A-only preset. Saved classes are not recalculated after filtering and differ from the 122-product full-data concentration finding.

Top 20 / Top 50 are display limits for the loaded scope. Online Demo sales sorting uses the latest 28 days; local Streamlit sales sorting uses all 97 days. Risk and exposure use June 5–July 2 in both. The Demo has 200 real series, so its lists and metrics are subset examples, not full-data conclusions.

The output is a review aid. It does not place orders, approve procurement or establish actual inventory targets. The responsible team must verify current stock, open orders and supplier inputs before taking an operational decision.

Scope & limitations

Sales are normalized and can be censored during stockouts. Only 97 days are available, so annual seasonality and long-term behavior cannot be established. Actual on-hand inventory, supplier lead times, purchase orders, procurement costs, revenue, shelf life and spoilage are unavailable. Replenishment scenarios use explicit assumptions; realized savings, financial ABC and recovered lost demand are not claimed.

Full analysis: 4.85M observations / 50,000 series. Committed demo: 19,400 real observations / 200 series, selected for exploration and not population-representative. Demo metrics are not full-data conclusions.

Forecasting with a clear evidence boundary

Compare naïve, weekly seasonal naïve, 28-day mean and simple exponential smoothing. Select each series’ model using three expanding-window validation folds within the first 90 days; evaluate the final seven-day holdout separately, then refit for an unscored future forecast.

The final holdout is reported honestly: per-series validation-selected forecasts achieved 37.4% pooled WAPE; a fixed simple-exponential-smoothing baseline achieved 36.0%. The simpler baseline performed better on this holdout, so model selection is not presented as an accuracy improvement.
