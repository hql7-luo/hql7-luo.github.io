Problem

The question worth solving.

Which store–product series show recurring availability exposure, and what evidence should guide replenishment review?

The source contains real sales and stockout observations, but no on-hand inventory, supplier or purchase-order records. I designed the analysis around those available fields: observed-sales forecasting, availability prioritization and explicitly assumed inventory scenarios.

Forecasting with a clear evidence boundary

Compare naïve, weekly seasonal naïve, 28-day mean and simple exponential smoothing. Select each series’ model using three expanding-window validation folds within the first 90 days; evaluate the final seven-day holdout separately, then refit for an unscored future forecast.

The final holdout is reported honestly: per-series validation-selected forecasts achieved 37.4% pooled WAPE; a fixed simple-exponential-smoothing baseline achieved 36.0%. The simpler baseline performed better on this holdout, so model selection is not presented as an accuracy improvement.

Management recommendation

From findings to a practical review workflow

The dashboard connects recurring stockout exposure with observed sales volume and variability. Management can filter the review scope, inspect the highest-priority series, compare validated forecasting methods, and test how assumed demand changes, lead times and service targets affect illustrative inventory requirements.

Explore the browser demo using 200 real store–product series (19,400 observations). Its metrics apply only to that subset. The full six-tab Streamlit dashboard remains available locally for the reproducible full analysis.

Availability alerts call for investigation and review. Replenishment outputs are scenario calculations on a normalized sales scale; executing an inventory decision requires actual stock positions, open orders and measured supplier lead times.

Scope & limitations

Sales are normalized and can be censored during stockouts. Only 97 days are available, so annual seasonality and long-term behavior cannot be established. Actual on-hand inventory, supplier lead times, purchase orders, procurement costs, revenue, shelf life and spoilage are unavailable. Replenishment scenarios use explicit assumptions; realized savings, financial ABC and recovered lost demand are not claimed.

Full analysis: 4.85M observations / 50,000 series. Committed demo: 19,400 real observations / 200 series, selected for exploration and not population-representative. Demo metrics are not full-data conclusions.
