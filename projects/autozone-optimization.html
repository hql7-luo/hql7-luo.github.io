AutoZone Workforce Optimization & Demand Forecasting

Built an Excel Solver workforce model for driver hiring and training under mileage-demand and target-headcount constraints, with a seasonal exponential-smoothing forecast of freight volume.

My role
Condensed a year of weekly freight records into 13 four-week periods, then built the Excel Solver model on them: how many drivers to hire or let go each period at the lowest wage, hiring and training cost, while covering each period's miles and keeping headcount at or above AutoZone's target.

Business problem

The question worth solving.

How can staffing and training decisions meet freight demand while reducing modeled workforce cost?

Approach

From problem to system.

Translate freight demand into capacity requirements → optimize hiring and training under mileage and headcount constraints → inform future staffing scenarios with forecasts.

  1. Translate freight demand into capacity requirements
  2. Optimize hiring and training under mileage and headcount constraints
  3. Inform future staffing scenarios with forecasts

Findings & recommendations

Model and decision

~$142.7K lower modeled FY25 labor cost vs. actual staffing
Optimized plan: 38–43 drivers across 13 four-week periods
Exponential smoothing · α = 0.75 · MAPE = 6.3%

Re-planning fiscal 2025 with the model covered every period's miles at about $142.7K less than the center's actual staffing. For the forecast, we combined a seasonal factor for each four-week period (from 2020–2025 data) with simple exponential smoothing at α = 0.75, so it tracked recent changes.

Individual extension

What I added after the course

After the course, I tested my own rule of thumb: keep one spare driver for sick days. In a weekly simulation (simulated data), overtime covered most absences. The real gaps came from summer volume peaks, so keeping 2 extra drivers all year and adding a third only for peak season met a 5% outsourcing target for about $36K a year less than keeping 3 all year.
