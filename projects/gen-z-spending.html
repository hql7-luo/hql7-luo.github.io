Gen Z Consumer Spending Analytics

Used SQL to analyze Gen Z income, spending and shopping preferences (about 1,700 financial records plus a separate shopping dataset), then turned the patterns into marketing recommendations.

My role
Queried and cleaned about 1,700 Gen Z consumer records in SQL, analyzed income, spending and consumption behavior, compared self-investment (education, fitness) with enjoyment (travel, entertainment) spending, used R, Python and Excel for analysis and charts, and worked out the business implications.

Business problem

The question worth solving.

How do Gen Z consumers earn, spend and shop, and what should that mean for marketing to them?

Approach

From problem to system.

Query and clean the records in SQL → compare income, spending and category mix → turn the patterns into marketing recommendations.

  1. Query and clean the records in SQL
  2. Compare income, spending and category mix
  3. Turn the patterns into marketing recommendations

Findings & recommendations

Spending patterns

50.6% of records spent more than their income
Income and spending barely move together: r = 0.03
Self-investment 13.48% vs enjoyment 14.19%

Spending barely followed income. Self-investment (education, fitness) and enjoyment (travel, entertainment) took similar shares of all spending, savings and investments. The team presented marketing recommendations based on these patterns.

Scope & limitations

The financial data is a public Kaggle file whose collection method is not documented, so the results describe this file, not all Gen Z consumers.

Individual extension

What I added after the course

  • Rebuilt the analysis in SQLite after the original files were lost, and checked every number again in Python. Both headline results reproduce exactly: r = 0.03, and 860 of 1,700 records (50.6%) spend more than their income.
  • While reproducing it, I also checked the data: all 14 numeric columns are spread evenly between round limits and no two correlate above 0.06, so the file looks randomly generated. The results describe this file, not real Gen Z behavior.
  • Spending above income turned out to be mostly an income effect: average spending is about $4,800 in every income decile, so 88% of below-median earners spend more than they earn, versus 14% of everyone else. With real data, I would segment customers on what they buy and A/B test one change at a time.
