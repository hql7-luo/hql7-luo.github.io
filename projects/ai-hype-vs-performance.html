AI Talk vs. AI Results

More AI talk.
Better results?

A study of 3,910 SEC 10-K filings from 498 current S&P 500 issuers, linking AI language to next-year revenue growth and stock returns.

What the evidence says

More AI language is associated with stronger subsequent revenue growth. The stock-return association changes across model choices, and the main models do not establish a changed relationship after ChatGPT.

FY2018–2026 · Evidence cutoff: October 26, 2026 · Observational, not causal

Business problem

A popular narrative is not a performance measure

Since ChatGPT’s release, AI has become a common theme in company disclosures. I wanted to test whether companies that discuss it more subsequently grow faster or deliver higher returns than SPY—and whether the association changed after November 30, 2022.

Mention counts measure language, including risks and boilerplate. They do not directly measure AI investment, adoption quality, or value created.

What I did

From original filings to a reproducible panel

  1. Start with 20 companies to validate the full pipeline, then expand the current S&P 500 universe. Deduplicate share classes by issuer and record coverage gaps.
  2. Cache original SEC submissions, 10-K main documents and companyfacts. Count a transparent AI dictionary per 10,000 visible-text words; standalone uppercase AI is case-sensitive.
  3. Reconcile annual revenue tags without adding overlapping concepts. Match original filings and adjacent fiscal periods; use identical adjusted stock/SPY trading endpoints.
  4. Compare disclosure groups and estimate industry/year fixed-effects models, controlling for revenue size and prior growth, with standard errors clustered by issuer. Check firm effects and outlier sensitivity.

Key findings

Disclosure increased. Return evidence is less stable.

For returns, the slope becomes −0.242 percentage points with firm and year effects (p=0.708), and +0.729 after 1st/99th-percentile winsorization (p=0.074). Both intervals include zero. The main interaction tests do not establish a slope change after ChatGPT: p=0.201 for growth and p=0.911 for returns.

Methods & limitations

What the study can—and cannot—tell us

  • Correlation, not causation. Management incentives, business model, R&D, growth opportunities and reverse causality can affect both language and outcomes. Controls do not remove all those differences.

  • Current-member selection. The sample uses current S&P 500 membership, creating survivorship bias. It does not represent all firms that existed in each historical year.

  • Measurement varies. Main-document text excludes incorporated annual reports and exhibits. Revenue concepts can include other income or be net of interest expense; industry effects do not make them identical accounting measures.

  • Timing and missing outcomes. Of all discovered filings, 498 lack a mature 12-month return window and two lack a starting price endpoint. Revenue and return regressions have separate complete-case samples. Full exclusion ledgers are retained.

Validation: 48 data and dictionary checks passed. Selected source checks matched original-accession financial facts and independent AI counts for 10 filings; this is a spot check, not a full source audit.
