NYC Rideshare Economics: Uber vs Lyft

How much do Uber and Lyft keep from each New York ride?

Built on official NYC Taxi & Limousine Commission trip records: every Uber and Lyft trip from February 2019 to August 2026, 91 months and 1.62 billion trips after cleaning.

The short answer

In August 2026, Uber kept 21.8% of the base fare after paying the driver ($5.98 a trip) and Lyft kept 24.1% ($6.16). This is gross take before insurance, payment costs and marketing, not profit.

The two platforms started far apart. In early 2019 Lyft kept about 30% and Uber close to nothing; since 2024 both have stayed between about 19% and 28%.

What I did

I chose the question, the metrics and the comparisons, and checked the results against outside numbers.

Business problem

Fares went up. Who kept the difference?

New York requires Uber and Lyft to report every trip to the Taxi & Limousine Commission, including the passenger's base fare and the driver's pay. That makes it one of the few places where anyone can see how the money from a ride is split.

The city added a driver minimum-pay rule in 2019 and a $1.50 congestion charge on rideshare trips into Manhattan below 60th Street in January 2025. I wanted to know how much each platform keeps, on which trips, and what changed around the congestion zone.

What I did

My Approach

  1. Process 91 monthly files of about 20 million trips each, one month at a time: download, summarize with DuckDB SQL, then delete the raw file. Only small summary tables are kept.
  2. Clean with simple rules (fare above zero, pay not negative, 0 to 100 miles, 1 to 240 minutes; 99.6% of rows passed), and define gross take as base fare minus driver pay.
  3. Compare take by company, month, trip length, hour, airport trips and pickup zone, plus congestion-zone trips vs. other trips in the same months of 2025 and 2024.
  4. Check against outside numbers: TLC's December 2025 trip count (22,108,438, matched exactly) and UCLA Labor Center's take-rate estimates (10.0% vs. 9.0% in Feb 2019, 21.1% vs. 20.7% in Apr 2022).

Gross take is what is left of the base fare after driver pay. It is not profit: insurance, payment processing, marketing and other costs are not in the trip data.

Key findings

What the Data Showed

24.1%Lyft's gross take rate in Aug 2026, vs. 21.8% for Uber
27.8%Lyft's share of Uber + Lyft trips, latest 12 months
30.4%Uber's take rate on trips up to 2 miles, vs. 18.2% for 5 to 10 miles
10.0%of Uber's trips up to 2 miles paid the driver more than the base fare
  • Airport trips were 8.4% of Uber's trips in the latest 12 months, with a 30.9% take rate and a 6.2-minute average wait.
  • By hour of the week, Uber's highest take rate was 29.8% on Tuesday at 21:00. Weekend evenings were the lowest.

Recommendations

If I were an Uber strategy analyst

  • Test price changes by zone and hour, and track completed trips, repeat riders and driver supply next to gross take. The past gap between fare and pay growth does not prove there is room to raise prices.
  • On short trips, improve pickup efficiency before raising minimum fares across the board. They keep a high share of the fare but little money per trip.
  • Use wait times to place drivers, and treat airport trips as their own market: 8.4% of trips with a 30.9% take rate.
  • Show the congestion charge separately from the platform's price, and watch demand, driver pay and take on zone trips separately. Only a controlled test can show who really bears the charge.

A descriptive analysis of public data. Gross take is not profit, and the comparisons do not prove causes.

Methods & limitations

How the numbers were built

Gross take = base passenger fare minus driver pay. Take rate = total take divided by total base fare, computed on summed totals. Neither includes tips, tolls, taxes or surcharges, and dollars are nominal.

The congestion zone is inferred from the data: Manhattan pickup zones with at least 1,000 trips in February 2025, more than 95% of which paid the charge. A trip counts as a zone trip if either end is in that set, so trips that only pass through the zone are missed.

Checks: 21 automated tests run on Python 3.11 and 3.12, the December 2025 trip count matches TLC exactly, and pooled take rates land within about one point of UCLA Labor Center's estimates.

Scope & limitations

Gross take is not profit, and driver bonuses or rider discounts may be missing from trip records. Trip mix, inflation, pay rules and seasons all affect the comparisons. The congestion comparison cannot show who finally bears the $1.50 charge, and the endpoint rule misses trips that only pass through the zone.
